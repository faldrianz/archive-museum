Nigeria has always made the future first. Faces were framed, rhythms were pressed, cloth was dyed and the street was drawn. We keep it all, so the past stays loud.
Room one

Photography

Studio portraits and street frames. Read hair, posture and light as a record of who people chose to be.

Room two

Music

Horns, long grooves and protest lyrics. Play restored sessions from highlife to Afrobeat and beyond.

Room three

Fashion

Indigo adire, woven aso-oke and sharp tailoring. Every pattern is a sentence in a language of cloth.

Room four

Design

Independence-era posters, painters and architects who bent modernism into a Nigerian shape.

    Plan your visit

    When is ARCHIVE open?

    Always. The museum is online, so every room is open day and night from any phone.

    Does it cost anything?

    No. Every exhibition is free, because a shared past should not sit behind a ticket.

    How do I add my family's story?

    Use the form just below. Pick a room, write a few lines, and your memory joins the wall.

    Can schools and groups use it?

    Yes. Each room works as a short lesson. Start with the timeline slider, then open pieces in the collection.

    Is it accessible?

    Rooms can be swiped, keyed with the arrow keys, or tapped. Motion respects your reduced-motion setting, and every control has a text label.
